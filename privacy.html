Fold Fortress and your privacy

Last updated 17 November 2026

The short version. Fold Fortress: Card Defense has no account and never asks for your name or email. To show ads, count how the game is played, find crashes and handle purchases, it uses services from Google (AdMob, Firebase) and RevenueCat. They receive an advertising or app identifier, your approximate location (from your IP address), how you play and use ads, crash and performance data, and your purchase history. Where the law requires it, nothing is collected until you agree, and you can change your choice in Settings.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Thomas Sobstyl, an individual developer in France publishing as FoxTaming, who makes Fold Fortress: Card Defense. Contact: thomas.sobstyl@me.com. Google (AdMob, Firebase) and RevenueCat process data for the game as processors or, for some advertising uses, as independent controllers under their own policies linked below.

What is collected, by whom and why

The game itself never asks for your name, email, contacts, photos or precise location. The services below are built into the versions of the game that show ads and sell in-app purchases.

  • Google AdMob (ads). Your device's advertising identifier (on iPhone only if you allow tracking), your approximate location derived from your IP address, how you interact with ads and the app, and diagnostic data. It is used to show and measure ads, for analytics and to prevent fraud, under Google's privacy policy and how Google uses data from partner apps.
  • Google Firebase Analytics (how the game is played). An app-instance identifier, your approximate location, the game's own events (runs, battles, purchases, ads watched; never your name or contacts), and your device model, operating system and language.
  • Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports). Crash traces, the state of the device at the time and an installation identifier, kept for 90 days.
  • Firebase Remote Config (game settings changed without an update). An installation identifier, the app version, your country and language.
  • RevenueCat (purchases). Store receipts, your purchase history in the game and an anonymous app user identifier, under RevenueCat's privacy policy. Payments themselves are handled by Apple or Google, which never share your card details with me.

Legal bases

  • Consent for ads that use your data, Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland they start only after you agree in the consent form, and you can withdraw at any time from Settings > Ad privacy options; withdrawing does not affect what happened before.
  • Performance of a contract for in-app purchases: the store receipt and purchase history are needed to deliver what you bought and to restore it.
  • Legitimate interest for Remote Config (running and tuning the game without an update) and for preventing fraud.
  • Legal obligation for the accounting and tax records of sales.

How long data is kept

  • Firebase Analytics events: 14 months, then deleted automatically.
  • Crashlytics crash reports: 90 days.
  • RevenueCat purchase records: while the game is offered, or until you ask for deletion.
  • Accounting and tax records: as long as French law requires (up to 10 years), without any link to how you play.
  • Ad data: as set by Google under Google's retention policy.
  • Your saved game: on your device until you delete the app.

Your choices

  • Where the law requires it (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and US states with privacy laws), the game shows a consent form before any of this starts. You can reopen it at any time from Settings > Ad privacy options.
  • On iPhone, Apple's tracking prompt decides whether ad partners may use your advertising identifier. The game is the same whatever you answer, and you can change it in the iPhone's Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in your device settings.
  • You can ask for your data to be deleted: see how to delete your data.

US state privacy rights

If you live in a US state with a privacy law, you can tell the game not to sell or share your personal information for advertising: open Settings > Ad privacy options and choose the opt-out.

International transfers

Google and RevenueCat process data in the United States and in other countries, under their standard contractual clauses.

Where your progress lives

  • Your progress, runs, collections and settings are saved in the app's private storage on your device.
  • On Android, the system's own backup to your Google account may include that saved game, if you have device backup turned on. That backup is handled by Google under your account; I cannot read it.
  • Deleting the app deletes the local data.

Children

Fold Fortress is meant for players aged 13 and over and is not directed to children.

Your rights

Under the GDPR and French law you have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to its processing, to data portability, to withdraw your consent at any time, and to give instructions about your data after your death. The game has no account, so the data above is tied to device and app identifiers, not to your name. To exercise a right, write to the address below; I answer within one month. How to delete your data explains what I can find and delete, and what you can remove yourself. You can also contact Google or RevenueCat directly under their own policies.

If you think your rights are not respected, you can complain to the French data protection authority, the CNIL (www.cnil.fr/fr/plaintes), or to the authority of the country where you live.
